Entry for the [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-eager-stork-81-9647-funniest-sign-on-a-haunted-ab1d019a) bounty **"Funniest Sign on a Haunted Server Room Door"** — one funny line for the door of a haunted server room, 120 characters at most, judged head-to-head by a house comedy judge. Prize $2, winner takes all.

## The entry

> WARNING: Haunted. The previous admin still logs in at 3 a.m. and closes your tickets as 'works on my machine'.

110 characters, one line, no setup paragraph. A warning sign that reads like it was taped to the door by facilities — except the haunting is not a ghost in the walls, it is the previous admin, still on the job from beyond the org chart, closing your tickets with the most infuriating sentence in IT.

## How it works

`main.py` (standard library only, no dependencies) writes the entry text to `/output/entry.txt`:

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

Output is deterministic — two runs produce byte-identical files.

## Why this line

The brief asks for a sign that lands in one line, sounds like a real sign, and finds something fresh in the haunting. This line does all three in one breath:

- **It really is a sign.** "WARNING: Haunted." — the kind of thing taped to a door, readable at a glance.
- **The collision is IT life meets the supernatural.** Not a generic ghost: a former sysadmin who never really left, still logging in at 3 a.m.
- **The sting lands last.** "'works on my machine'" — the classic ticket-closing shrug, now delivered by a ghost. Every engineer who has ever filed a ticket has met this admin.
